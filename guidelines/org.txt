WIKIPEDIA NOTABILITY FOR ORGANIZATIONS AND COMPANIES (WP:CORP / WP:NCORP)

Organizations and companies are evaluated under the General Notability Guideline (WP:GNG), but there is a significantly higher burden of proof to prevent Wikipedia from being used as a corporate directory or for free advertising.

To demonstrate notability for a company or organization, the sources MUST:
1. Provide significant coverage specifically investigating, analyzing, or evaluating the company or its operations beyond basic factual reporting.
2. The coverage must be entirely independent of the company and not originate from their PR engine.

EXPLICIT EXCLUSIONS THAT DO NOT CONSTITUTE NOTABILITY:
A major failure often occurs when people submit 'Routine Business News' as evidence of notability. 

The following types of coverage are classified as "Routine" and MUST BE REJECTED entirely when evaluating notability:
- Announcements of new product launches, app updates, or service rollouts.
- Reports detailing funding rounds, capital raises, VC investments, IPOs, or "closing a $X million seed round." 
- Reports of corporate mergers and acquisitions (unless the M&A fundamentally reshaped the national economy or industry).
- Announcements of new office openings, factory expansions, or hiring executives/board members.
- Financial reports, quarterly earnings announcements, or changes in stock prices.
- Press releases verbatim, "churnalism," or syndicated content originating from public PR wires (e.g., PRNewswire, Business Wire, ANI, IANS, Reuters wire feeds that simply reprint unedited company statements). 
- Interviews with the CEO, founders, or executives where they discuss the company's vision or performance. (Because the company controls the narrative).
- Industry awards, "Top 100 Companies" listicles, or basic profile directory listings (e.g., Crunchbase, Pitchbook).

WHAT DOES CONSTITUTE NOTABILITY:
- An independent journalistic investigation into the company's business practices or history by a major news organization (e.g., a massive deep-dive feature in The Wall Street Journal or Bloomberg Businessweek).
- Exhaustive books published by independent authors focusing heavily on the organization's rise or cultural impact.
- Case studies from credible academic institutions analyzing the unique market strategy of the organization.
- Meaningful external commentary: The company must be shown to have produced a widely notable cultural, economic, or historical impact verified by independent third-party analysts, rather than just surviving or growing financially.
